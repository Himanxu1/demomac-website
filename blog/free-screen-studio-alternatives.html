Free Screen Studio alternatives for Mac: what you actually get for $0

Screen Studio doesn't have a free plan, so a lot of people go looking for a free Screen Studio alternative. There are good ones — but "free" always comes with a catch: a personal-use licence, a five-minute limit, or a pile of manual editing. Here's what each free option gives you, and what it doesn't.

Alternatives20 October 2026 · 3 min read

What makes Screen Studio worth paying for

To judge a free alternative fairly, be clear about what you're replacing. Screen Studio's value is the editing it does on its own:

  1. Zooms that follow your clicks, without keyframing.
  2. A smoothed cursor.
  3. A background, padding and rounded corners, so the recording looks designed.
  4. Easy export to the right size for each platform.

A free tool that records but does none of these isn't really an alternative — it's a starting point for an hour in a video editor.

1. Cap (free plan) — the closest free match

Catch: personal use only; share links capped at 5 minutes.

Cap is open source and has a Studio Mode with a full editor: zooms, backgrounds, padding. You place and adjust zooms yourself rather than having them placed for you, but the look is close. For side projects and personal videos, it's the best free answer. For anything commercial, you need the $29/year or $58 lifetime licence.

2. OBS Studio — free with no limits

Catch: no polish at all, and a steep learning curve.

OBS records any screen, window or app, at any length, with no watermark, and can capture Mac system audio with an audio capture source. It does nothing to the footage: no zooms, no cursor smoothing, no background. Great for raw capture and live streaming.

3. The macOS built-in recorder — already on your Mac

Catch: no system audio, no editing beyond a trim.

Press Shift-Command-5 to record the whole screen or a selection, with your microphone. It's perfect for a quick bug report. You'll get a raw .mov with a jumpy cursor and no zoom. (QuickTime Player's "New Screen Recording" is the same recorder.)

4. Loom (free plan) — fast, but limited

Catch: 5-minute recordings, 25 videos, and everything is uploaded.

Loom's free Starter plan is great for a quick message to a teammate. It isn't built for polished demos and has no auto zoom.

5. Open-source auto-zoom projects

A handful of open-source recorders on GitHub now add auto zoom and cursor smoothing. They change fast, so check how recently each one was updated and whether it supports your macOS version before you build a workflow on it.

6. "Free to try" paid apps

Sometimes the best free option is a paid app with a generous free tier. ScreenToast is free to record and edit with no time limit and no sign-in: Auto Polish adds the zooms and smooths the cursor, and you can edit as much as you like. You only pay ($59 a year, or $7 for a month) when you export. If you want to see whether the Screen Studio look is worth paying for, it's a no-risk way to find out.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free version of Screen Studio?

No. Screen Studio has no free plan as of 20 October 2026 — only $29 a month or $108 a year.

What is the best free Screen Studio alternative?

Cap is the closest: it's open source and its Studio Mode has a zoom editor and backgrounds. Its free plan is for personal use only. For raw recording with no polish, OBS Studio is free with no limits.

Can I use a free screen recorder for business videos?

Check the licence. OBS Studio and the macOS built-in recorder have no commercial restrictions. Cap's free plan is personal use only; its paid Desktop licence adds commercial rights.
