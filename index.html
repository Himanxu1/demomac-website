The flow

Four steps, and three of them happen without you.

Most screen recorders hand you a raw file and a timeline. ScreenToast hands you a finished demo, then lets you change your mind about any part of it.

01 — YOU

Record

Whole display, one window, or an area locked to the shape you publish in. Camera, mic and system audio if you want them.

02 — AUTO

Polish

The zooms get placed where you clicked, a flurry of clicks becomes one smooth move, and the cursor stops jittering.

03 — AUTO

Narrate

04 — YOU

Publish

While you record

Get the take right before there is anything to fix.

The things you cannot fix later — the shape of the frame, what's on screen behind you — are the ones ScreenToast sorts out before you hit record.

01 — AREA RECORDING

Pick the shape before you record, not after.

Crop a wide recording down to a vertical clip and you throw away most of the picture — and you can never get it back. ScreenToast holds your selection to the shape you're posting in, so what you record is what you publish.

  • Six shapes. Free, 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 4:5 and 4:3 — press 1–6 to switch without letting go of the mouse.
  • The shape never slips however you drag it, and the box can't wander off the edge of your screen.
  • It opens ready to post. Choose 9:16 and the editor is already set up for TikTok and Reels — nothing left to configure.
  • Edges stay crisp, so your text is as sharp in the export as it was on screen.

02 — CAMERA

Your face, framed for the platform you post to.

Your camera sits in a neat circle wherever you want it, and can move during the demo. For vertical video, ScreenToast stacks your face above the screen and slides the screen along to keep the action in view.

  • Eight spots to choose from — any corner or edge — and you can resize it to taste.
  • Made for phones. A wide desktop recording becomes something people can actually read on a phone, not a thin strip in the middle.
  • Change your mind later. Move it, resize it or remove your camera entirely after you've stopped recording.

Auto Polish

The part that normally takes an hour in an editor.

ScreenToast pays attention to what you did, not just what the screen looked like. Every click, every pause, every drag — that's how it knows where the interesting moments are.

03 — SMART ZOOM

Zooms that land on what you clicked.

ScreenToast notices where you clicked and how long you lingered, then zooms in on the moments worth looking at. Six quick clicks in the same corner become one smooth move, not six jolts.

  • Disagree with any of it. Every zoom lands on the timeline as something you can drag, retime, soften or delete.
  • Start over any time. Run it again and it redoes its work cleanly instead of piling zooms on top of zooms.
  • Never shows the edge of your recording, so you'll never get a black bar where the picture ran out.

04 — CURSOR

A calm pointer that still arrives on time.

Other tools tidy up your cursor while you record, which leaves the pointer visibly trailing behind your clicks. ScreenToast waits until you've finished and smooths the whole path at once — so the movement is calm and the click still lands exactly on the button.

  • No drifting behind. Your pointer arrives when you meant it to.
  • It never gets mushy. Polish the same recording ten times and the cursor looks as crisp as it did the first time.
  • Nothing gets dropped while you work, however busy your Mac gets.

05 — FOLLOW CURSOR

A zoom that travels with you.

Hold a zoom while you drag a slider, move a card across a board or scroll a long page, and the frame follows the pointer instead of sitting still and losing it off the edge.

  • It ignores the small stuff. Little twitches don't wobble the picture; it only moves when you actually go somewhere.
  • Switches itself on when you start dragging, and it's one toggle everywhere else.
  • Follow you into a corner and the frame still stays full — no edges, no gaps.

06 — THE LOOK

Backgrounds and device frames that survive 4K.

Drop your recording onto a gradient, a plain colour or one of your own photos, then sit it inside a MacBook or iMac. Everything stays razor sharp, right up to 4K.

  • Adjust the padding, rounding and shadow until it looks right. Nothing is locked in.
  • Blur a busy photo so your background sets a mood without stealing attention.
  • The preview keeps up while you're deciding — no waiting for it to catch you up.

In the editor

Everything is a decision you can undo.

ScreenToast never touches your original recording. Everything you change sits on top of it, so there's no such thing as a decision you can't take back — today or six months from now.

07 — PRIVACY

Blur the thing you shouldn't have shown.

Drag a blur over an email address, a customer's name or a password field. It sticks to the thing it's covering, so when a zoom moves the picture the blur goes with it.

  • Blur as many things as you need, dragging and resizing each one straight on the preview.
  • Choose how strong each one is, from a light frost to completely unreadable.
  • Tidy up first. Hide your desktop icons and silence notifications before you start, so there's less to blur at all.

08 — TITLES

An intro before, a call to action after.

Open with your product name and close with where to find you. Adding an intro later never knocks any of your zooms out of place.

  • Set how long each one lasts, with its own colour and the way the text arrives.
  • Point things out with text and callouts on the recording itself, right where you put them.
  • A watermark worth keeping on free exports — and none at all once you buy.

09 — TIMELINE

Trim the dead air, speed up the boring middle.

Drag the ends in to trim, speed up the part where you're just waiting for something to load, mute anything you don't want. The sound stays in step with the picture instead of sliding out three seconds later.

  • Cut a chunk out and everything after it closes up neatly, zooms and all.
  • Your voice, the app's sound and the music sit on separate tracks, so you can mute or level each one on its own.
  • Undo actually works. Nudging a slider is one step back, not forty.

10 — VOICEOVER

It writes the script from what you actually did.

ScreenToast looks at what you did on screen, writes narration that follows along, and reads it aloud in a voice you choose. It's just text — rewrite any of it before a single word is spoken.

  • It lands on the right moment. Each line sits with the thing it's describing, and they never talk over each other.
  • Prefer your own voice? Record it, and the music quietens itself underneath automatically.
  • Cleans up your microphone, so a laptop mic stops sounding like a laptop mic.

11 — EXPORT

Re-framed, not just letterboxed.

Switch from wide to vertical and ScreenToast re-frames the picture instead of chopping the sides off. The crop follows the action, so the thing you're demonstrating stays in shot.

  • Ready-made settings for YouTube, TikTok, Reels, LinkedIn, X and Product Hunt.
  • Save as a video or a GIF, including a high-quality master if you're taking it into another editor.
  • Straight to your Mac. No upload, no queue, no link that quietly expires.

Questions

Before you download.

How is this different from Screen Studio or Screen Charm?

Three things. It's a fraction of the size — 7.7 MB against several hundred — and opens instantly. It writes and speaks your narration, which neither of them does. And it records in the shape you're posting in, so a vertical clip is filmed vertical rather than cropped out of a wide one afterwards.

Does anything I record get uploaded?

No. Recording, editing and saving all happen on your Mac. The one exception is the spoken narration: if you use it, the words you approve are sent away to be turned into speech. Your video is never part of that — only the text.

Are there captions?

Not yet — they're what we're building next, and they'll arrive as a free update. Everything else on this page works today.

Can I still edit what Auto Polish did?

All of it. Every zoom lands on the timeline as something you can drag, retime, soften or delete. And if you'd rather start fresh, running it again redoes its work cleanly instead of piling more on top.

What can it export?

Video or GIF, in whatever shape you need. There are ready-made settings for YouTube, TikTok, Reels, LinkedIn, X and Product Hunt, plus a high-quality master if you're taking it into another editor — or pick your own size.

Can I record system audio and my microphone at once?

Yes, and they stay on separate tracks so you can mute or adjust each one afterwards. Your camera is kept separate too, which is what lets you move it or drop it once you've finished.

What happens if my Mac crashes mid-recording?

Your footage is saved as you go, not held until you press stop. Open ScreenToast again and it offers you everything up to the moment things went wrong.

Is there a Windows version?

No, and there isn't going to be one. ScreenToast is built specifically for the Mac, and that's exactly why it's this small and this fast. Making it work everywhere would cost the thing that makes it good.

What do I need to run it?

macOS 14 or later, on any Mac from the last few years. ScreenToast asks permission to record your screen, and for your microphone and camera only if you switch them on.

Can I use it on more than one Mac?

Up to three Macs that you own, on the one licence. If you replace a machine, deactivate the old one from the app and the seat comes back.
