Screen recorders with auto zoom: 6 tools compared

Auto zoom is the feature that turned screen recordings from "a video of my desktop" into something that looks produced. A screen recorder with auto zoom tracks where you click and zooms the frame in on those moments, no keyframes required. But the tools differ a lot in how they choose zooms and how much control you keep. Here's how six of them compare.

How auto zoom works

While you record, the app logs every click, keystroke and cursor position alongside the video. After you stop, it scores those events — where you clicked, how long you lingered, whether clicks cluster — and turns the important ones into zoom events. The quality differences come down to:

  • Merging: does a flurry of clicks become one smooth move, or six jolts?
  • Edges: does it ever zoom past the edge of the recording and show black?
  • Following: when you drag a slider or scroll, does the zoom move with you?
  • Editability: can you move, retime and delete individual zooms?
  • Re-running: if you run it again, does it stack zooms on zooms?

The six tools

1. ScreenToast

Scores your clicks and how long you lingered, merges bursts into one move, and never zooms past the frame edge. Four zoom types: Auto from clicks, Manual on one spot, Follow that travels with your pointer (switched on automatically when you drag), and a Glass Loupe that magnifies just a lens. Every zoom is a timeline block you can drag, retime, soften or delete, and re-running Auto Polish replaces its own work cleanly. Mac, $59/year to export, free to try.

2. Screen Studio

The tool that made auto zoom mainstream. Automatic or manual zooms with smooth easing. Mac, $108/year.

3. Screen Charm

Smart auto-zoom applied after recording, with a smooth cursor. Mac, $99 one-time at launch pricing.

4. FocuSee

Automatic zoom and cursor effects on both Mac and Windows. From $49.99/year.

5. CleanShot X

Smart zooms in its video editor — lighter than the dedicated tools but good for short clips. Mac, $35 once.

6. Cap

A zoom editor in Studio Mode; open source, Mac and Windows. Free for personal use, $58 lifetime for commercial.

Auto zoom tips

  • Click deliberately. Auto zoom keys off clicks; a pause before each click helps the app pick the right moments.
  • Don't over-zoom. 1.5–2× is plenty for most UIs. Delete zooms on trivial clicks.
  • Leave a beat after a zoom so the viewer can read before the next move.
