How to record your screen and webcam at the same time on Mac

A face in the corner makes a tutorial or demo feel human. macOS doesn't have a "screen plus camera" button, but there are two good ways to record your screen and webcam on a Mac: a free trick with QuickTime, and a recorder that keeps the camera on its own track so you can change it later.

Method 1: the free QuickTime trick

  1. Open QuickTime Player → File → New Movie Recording. Your webcam appears.
  2. Make the window small and drag it into a corner. Choose View → Float on Top so it stays above other windows.
  3. Press Shift-Command-5 and record the whole screen (or an area including the camera window).

The catch: the camera is just a window in your recording. It's a rectangle with window chrome, it's in your way when you click near it, and you can't move, resize or remove it afterwards.

Method 2: a camera on its own track (ScreenToast)

ScreenToast records your camera separately from the screen, which is what makes it editable later.

  1. In the recorder bar, switch on Camera (and Mic), and pick your devices.
  2. Choose Display, Window or Area and record. Stop with ⌘⇧2.
  3. In the editor, open the Camera tool and choose a layout:
    • Bubble — a circle or rounded shape in any of eight spots. It slides to another corner when your cursor heads its way, so it never covers what you're showing.
    • Side by side — you and the screen together.
    • Camera only — for an intro or outro talking to the viewer.
    • Screen only — hide the camera for a section.
  4. Switch layouts part-way through — for example, camera only for the intro, then a bubble for the demo.
  5. Optional: cut out the background behind you, on your Mac, and pick one of twelve colour looks (or your own LUT).

For vertical video, ScreenToast can stack your face above the screen so it's readable on a phone instead of being a thin strip.

Other apps that do this

  • Loom — camera bubble, uploaded to the cloud; free up to 5 minutes.
  • Screen Studio — several camera layouts including a cut-out; $108/year.
  • OBS Studio — free; add your camera as a source in your scene. Fixed once recorded unless you record it to a separate file.

Tips for looking good on camera

  • Light your face from the front. A window or lamp behind your screen, not behind you.
  • Camera at eye level. Prop your laptop up on a few books.
  • Look at the lens when you speak to the viewer, at the screen when you're demonstrating.
  • Keep the bubble small during the demo; the product is the star.
