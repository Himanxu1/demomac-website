Top 5 screen recording apps for macOS, picked by who you are

Most "best screen recorder" lists give you fifteen apps and leave you to work it out. This one gives you the top 5 screen recording apps for macOS — one per kind of user — and tells you which to choose. (Want the longer list? See the 10 best screen recorders for Mac.)

{{COVER}}

1. For founders and marketers: ScreenToast

You make: launch videos, landing-page demos, feature announcements, social clips.

Why: you don't have time to learn a video editor, and the video has to look professional. ScreenToast records your real product and polishes it automatically — zooms on your clicks, a smooth cursor, a background — and if you don't want to talk, it drafts narration from what you did on screen. Record a 9:16 area for Reels and TikTok without cropping, and export presets for YouTube, LinkedIn, X and Product Hunt from the same project.

Price: free to record and edit; $59/year or $7/month to export.

2. For teachers and course creators: ScreenFlow

You make: long lessons with many sections, slides and callouts.

Why: long-form teaching needs a real timeline — chapters, layered media, precise cuts. ScreenFlow is the Mac's best all-in-one recorder and editor for that.

Price: $199 once.

3. For developers and designers: CleanShot X

You make: screenshots, GIFs for pull requests, 20-second bug repros.

Why: it's the fastest way to capture anything on your screen and annotate it, and its recorder handles quick clips and GIFs with system audio.

Price: $35 once.

4. For teams: Loom

You make: async updates, code reviews, customer walkthroughs.

Why: the link is ready the moment you stop, with comments and view notifications. Nothing beats it for internal messages.

Price: free (5-minute limit, 25 videos); $18/user/month for Business.

5. For a $0 budget: the built-in recorder (or OBS)

You make: the occasional recording.

Why: Shift-Command-5 is free and instant. If you need system audio, OBS Studio is free and records it.

Price: free.
